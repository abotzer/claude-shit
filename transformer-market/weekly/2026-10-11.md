# NA Transformer Market Weekly: Oct 5–11, 2026

*First run. This report sets the baseline in `market-map.md`. "Changed this week" means events dated Oct 5–11, 2026. Older items that matter are listed separately under "Last six weeks".*

## TL;DR

1. **Refurbished equipment and rental fleets are now acquisition targets.** IPS bought Belyea's high-voltage refurbishment and portable-substation business (effective **Oct 7**). It is the third such deal since July, after TJC → Sunbelt Solomon and MacLean/Blackstone → Delta Star. Investors are paying for anything that can deliver a transformer in under two years.
2. **India is starting to fill the space China is losing.** Marsons (India, 11–345 kV) and NY private-equity firm Cleanhill approved a US/Canada JV on **Oct 6**. Marsons stock rose 16%. The JV imports first and talks about local manufacturing later.
3. **Domestic electrical steel (GOES) is loosening, but only a little.** Cleveland-Cliffs showed Energy Secretary Wright (**Oct 6**) a $200M Butler Works expansion backed by a $75M DOE award, adding up to **+25%** GOES output. Cliffs is still the only US maker, and its widths are too narrow for many large-transformer cores.
4. **The rule on Chinese grid equipment is now in DOE's hands.** Comments on DOE's request for information under EO 14420 closed **Oct 9**. Rules are due **~Dec 24**. The biggest open question is whether data-center substations count as "bulk-power". Data centers are where Chinese transformers were filling the lead-time gap.
5. **Overall:** demand and policy still favor domestic production. The risk is timing: the incumbents' capacity wave arrives in 2027–29, and price growth has slowed to single digits year over year.

## What this implies if you want to build transformers

### 1. Policy is pushing the cheapest competitor out, and someone else will step in
EO 14420 targets Chinese equipment. Wood Mackenzie says data centers have been using Chinese units to work around lead times, with more than $22B of bulk-power imports exposed since 2025 and the 100 MVA+ segment hit hardest ([WoodMac](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/us-executive-order-banning-chinese-bulk-power-equipment-set-to-worsen-critical-transformer-shortage/)).

If DOE's December rules cover customer-owned and data-center substations, that demand moves to domestic and allied suppliers. That helps a US builder.

The **Marsons JV shows that import competition will move, not disappear.** Indian makers are not on the 24-country list. Today they face only the Section 232 derivative tariff (25%, or 15% for large liquid-filled units under the disputed narrow reading). Plan your pricing assuming Indian, Korean and Mexican competition, not Chinese.

### 2. The quickest way in is the aftermarket, and that is what investors are buying
Three deals in three months: IPS–Belyea, TJC–Sunbelt Solomon, MacLean–Delta Star. All three are in refurbishment, rental, mobile substations or lifecycle services. The reasons:
- A refurbished or rental unit avoids the 2–4 year wait.
- These shops need far less capex than a greenfield power-transformer plant.
- They avoid most of the multi-year utility qualification cycle.

If you are serious about building, a **repair, rewind and remanufacturing shop is the lower-risk way in**. It builds the same skills (winding, drying, testing) and the same utility relationships. It is also the asset private equity is paying for right now.

### 3. Supply of electrical steel is the gating item, not demand
- Cliffs adding up to 25% is welcome, but it is still one domestic supplier.
- Its widths are too narrow for many large-transformer cores.
- Imported cores and laminations carry Section 232 steel duties ([Breakthrough](https://www.breakthroughjournal.org/p/america-makes-the-wrong-steel-for)).
- Copper is near a record.

**Lock in core supply before you lock in a site.** Options include Cliffs, imported GOES with tariffs priced in, or contracted core makers such as Worthington's new Burlington, Ontario plant. Model input costs at today's tariff levels.

### 4. Timing is the main risk to your thesis
- **Most incumbent capacity arrives in 2027–29:** Eaton SC (2027), ERMCO AZ (2027), HD Hyundai #2 (Apr 2027), Siemens Energy Charlotte (2027), Prolec Goldsboro (by end-2027), VTC Muscle Shoals (Jan 2028), Hitachi South Boston (2028), Hitachi Gallman (2029). Most of these are brownfield projects by firms that already hold utility qualifications and test bays.
- **The shortage is shrinking because supply is responding.** Wood Mackenzie's power-transformer deficit went from 30% (2025) to 15% (2026), and one secondary source projects ~5% by 2030.
- **Prices are still rising, but more slowly.** The BLS transformer index is +91% since Jan 2020 but only +8.7% over the last year. On another series it is +4.3%.
- **A greenfield plant announced in 2027 ships first units around 2029, right into that wave.** VTC, an experienced incumbent, needs about 20 months from announcement to first production.

### 5. Where the gaps are likely to last
- **3-phase pad-mount and medium-voltage units for data centers and industry.** Wood Mackenzie expects this shortage to get *worse*. It is getting crowded, though: Eaton, ERMCO, JST, Giga, Forgent/VanTran and Hammond are all adding capacity.
- **Generator step-up (GSU) transformers.** They have the longest lead times (~144+ weeks), and gas-turbine backlogs (GE Vernova >100 GW) lock in demand into the 2030s. Mid-size GSUs (≤200 MVA) are the size Marsons is targeting.
- **Mobile substations, specialty and rectifier units, and test-bay capacity.** These are niches where incumbents are buying rather than building.
- **765 kV / extra-high-voltage.** Demand is real (ERCOT, PJM, MISO), but it needs a lot of capital and expertise. Only Hyosung, Mitsubishi, PTT and soon HD Hyundai can make these units. It is not a sensible place for a new entrant.

### 6. Prices for selling a transformer business are high
- Prolec GE: 50% for $5.25B, implying ~$10.5B for the whole company.
- Virginia Transformer sale talk: >$6B.
- Forgent: IPO at $27, follow-on at $47.
- Private equity now owns or is buying Delta Star, Sunbelt Solomon and IPS.

A **build-to-sell plan** over 4–6 years is credible if you win qualified utility or hyperscaler customers before the 2028–29 supply wave. **Buying an existing small maker** is expensive at these prices, but much faster.

**Bottom line:** the bull case still holds for 2026–28, helped by policy and a rising utility capex base ($239B in 2026, $1.4T over 2026–30). It now depends on choosing the right **segment** (pad-mount, MV, GSU, aftermarket rather than large power), arriving **early enough** (before 2028), and having **secured core steel**. Building just any transformer factory is no longer an obvious win.
