# North American Transformer Market: Deep Dive (2026-10-12)

*Two rounds of multi-agent research on government data, permits, incentive filings, dockets, company filings, trade statistics and utility procurement, with independent checks of the key claims. Confidence tags: P primary, S secondary, W weak. Bracketed ids point to findings in `research/round1_research.json` and `research/round2_research.json`.*

## Answer

**Answer: still build, but round 2 moves the target up one size class and narrows the route.**
- **Target:** substation transformers of 10-60 MVA (extendable to ~100) with 69-138 kV primaries. Customers are utility distribution substations, data-center main substations and solar/BESS collector substations. Run 3-phase pad-mounts (0.5-5 MVA) alongside as a fast first product and volume base, not as the thesis.
- **Route:** buy or partner into a plant that already has a test bay and utility qualifications, or add a 69-138 kV line inside an acquired distribution plant. Build pad-mount capacity in an existing building, not greenfield.
- **Timing:** commit after DOE's EO 14421 rules (~Dec 24-27, 2026) and the Q3 margin prints. Ship substation units in 2028-29.
- **Avoid:** large power, EHV and large GSUs (unchanged).

**Why the target moved**
- **EO scope:** EO 14421 (not 14420) covers bulk-power equipment at ≥69 kV with no MVA floor, and it excludes local distribution [bps-def-69kv, bps-equipment-def, P/S]. A grid-facing 20 MVA 138/34.5 kV unit is in scope; a utility pad-mount is not.
- **Lead times:** buyer records put the median quoted substation lead time at ~130 weeks (n=9, W) [calc-substation-lt-median]. New pad-mounts quoted 40-50 weeks in early 2026 (W), and one distribution order in August 2026 quoted 12-13 weeks [lt-reman-testimony-2026, bid-vermillion-2026].
- **Pricing power is clearest in power transformers:**
  - HD Hyundai earned a 25.1% operating margin while its North America revenue grew only 10%, so the gain came from price and mix.
  - Hyosung's margin rose 490 bp.
  - GE Vernova Electrification rose 700 bp organically [hdhe-q2, hdhe-na-orders, hyosung-q2, gev-elec-q2].
- **The 10-160 MVA wave is later and smaller than the headlines:**
  - Hitachi Gallman (2029) partly replaces Crystal Springs.
  - VTC Rincon slipped to +50% by March 2027.
  - Muscle Shoals shows no site work since its May announcement.
  - Niagara is still in IDA approval [se-gallman-replaces-crystal-springs, se-vtc-rincon-slip, se-vtc-ms-scope, niagara-ecida].
  - Two competitors are on schedule: Howard reaches 250 MVA/345 kV by end-2026 and Prolec Goldsboro reaches 420 units/yr by end-2027 [se-howard-ratings, prolec-gso-deadline].
- **Pad-mount is filling fast:**
  - New supply: Jinpan-owned JST (1,000 units/yr from 2027) [jst-is-jinpan-subsidiary]; PTT's pad-mount plant (early 2027) [ptt-raeford-padmount]; Eaton, ERMCO and Giga; and Forgent, which claims capacity of ~2x its FY27 revenue guide [fps-mix-capacity].
  - Chinese-owned Eaglerise has US and Mexico plants ramping, and it underbid US distributors by 18-23% on Lafayette's 3-phase pad-mount items [eaglerise-us-mexico-plants, bid-lafayette-2026].
  - Stated additions roughly match the computed data-center pull in ERCOT plus PJM of ~2,600-5,000 MV/LV units a year (W) [dc-load-to-units-computed].

**What supports the bull case**
- **Margins are rising:** Forgent's adjusted EBITDA margin was 24.4% in Q4 FY26 and is guided to ~24% for FY27. Hammond's was 16.4%, and Prolec earned ~25% before it sold [fps-fy26-results, fps-fy27-guide, hps-q2-26, gev-prolec-margin].
- **Prices are at records:** the main transformer PPI rose 4.6% in July alone (level re-confirmed) [verify-ppi-wpu117409], and buyers are paying PPI escalators (Ocala +$1.1M) [bid-ocala-ppi-2026].
- **Demand is now partly countable:**
  - ~27 ERCOT 765/345 kV transformer banks.
  - ~50-160 US GSUs a year.
  - 460-890 MV/LV units and 9-22 HV/MV units per GW of data-center load.
  - ERCOT has 9.5 GW approved to energize, and PJM forecasts +65.7 GW of peak by 2036.
- **Scale is rewarded:** Prolec cleared at ~12.9-14.1x 2025E EBITDA, against 5.9-8.8x for lower-middle-market manufacturing [prolec-implied-multiple, lmm-manufacturing-multiples].

**What weakens it**
- **Gross margins are not widening:** at distribution makers they are flat to down (Forgent ~36.9% to ~35.1%), and Hubbell Utility was flat. Hammond's backlog fell 6.9% QoQ. Q3 is the first quarter carrying the full rise in copper (+45%) and base oil (+111%) [margin-direction].
- **The EO mostly helps allies:** Korea (0-4.32% antidumping), Mexico, Brazil and India take most of the substitution, and nothing is banned until DOE makes findings [trigger-and-timing].
- **No federal money in the base case:** there is no DPA Title III solicitation, 48C is exhausted and EDF is untested [fed-dpa-no-solicitation, fed-48c-exhausted]. State and local incentives are 0.8-6.4% of capex [computed-incentive-intensity].
- **Labor:** seven plants hiring in 2027-28 announce 4,388 jobs, 1.2x the 3,600 coil winders in all of NAICS 3353 [announced-jobs-7-plants]. Contracted average wages are $65-72k.
- **Cores:** imported cores may pay 50% duty rather than 25% (unresolved), and Butler's new GOES capacity lands in 2028 [verify-s232-cores, verify-cliffs-butler].
- **Acquisition targets are thin:** Southwest Electric, Delta Star, PTT and Sunbelt Solomon are taken, and VTC asks more than $6B.

**Walk away if** (thresholds in section 8):
- DOE's rules set an MVA floor above ~100 MVA, broadly grandfather existing suppliers, or license Chinese-owned Mexican plants wholesale.
- Ten or more 2027 bid tabs show median quoted delivery under 20 weeks for 3-phase pad-mounts or under 52 weeks for substation units.
- Forgent's or Hammond's gross margin falls 2+ points for two quarters, or transformer PPI growth stays under 3% YoY for two quarters.
- By mid-2027 there is no core contract and no anchor offtake covering ≥40% of year-1 output, or the only qualified target costs more than 10x forward EBITDA at 2023-25 price growth.

**Caveat:** no plant date is verified by a permit. Buyer prices are mostly W-grade summaries of small-municipality minutes. Census HS-10 import units and QCEW employment are still blocked.

## What changed after the second research round

- Target moved up a size class. Round 1 led with 0.5-10 MVA pad-mounts and treated 10-100 MVA as phase 2. Round 2 makes 10-60 MVA units with 69-138 kV primaries the core target, entered through an acquired or brownfield plant with a test bay. Pad-mount becomes a fast phase-1 product. Three reasons: the EO text excludes local distribution, substation quotes run ~130 weeks against 40-50 for new pad-mounts, and pad-mount capacity is filling faster than round 1 counted.
- EO number corrected. It is EO 14421 (FR Doc 2026-17843, Aug 31, 2026), not 14420 [eo-number-14421]. Round 1 used 14420 throughout, and so do the baseline, the federal-programs stream and the ownership stream.
- EO scope now comes from the text, not the 2020 template:
  - The bulk-power system starts at ≥69 kV and local distribution is excluded; there is no MVA floor.
  - A 'Covered Foreign Entity' means the 24 ITAR 126.1 countries plus an owned/controlled/jurisdiction test.
  - It applies to transactions after Aug 26 and prohibits nothing until DOE finds undue risk.
  - Installed equipment is not grandfathered [bps-def-69kv, cfe-ownership-test, trigger-and-timing, installed-equipment].

  Round 1's '24 foreign adversary countries' framing was wrong: the list is ITAR 126.1, not the six-country 15 CFR 7.4 list.
- Round 1's walk-away condition 'DOE's rules exclude distribution' is now more likely than not to be met for utility pad-mounts. The pad-mount case no longer assumes any EO premium. The EO-related walk-away now applies to the 69-138 kV case instead.
- Margins reversed from 'unknown, probably squeezed' to 'expanding at EBITDA level through Q2 2026':
  - Forgent Q4 FY26: 24.4%, +640 bp.
  - HD Hyundai: 25.1%.
  - Hyosung: 15.7%, +490 bp.
  - GE Vernova Electrification: 18.4%, +390 bp reported, +700 bp organic.
  - Hammond: 16.4%.

  Gross margin is the exception. It slipped at Forgent (~36.9% to ~35.1%) and was flat at Hammond and at Hubbell Utility [margin-direction].
- Buyer-level prices and lead times now exist: ~45 datapoints, mostly W.
  - New 3-phase pad-mounts: $27.6-31.7/kVA at 1.5-2 MVA, $88.9/kVA at 225 kVA.
  - Single-phase units: ~$3,000 each.
  - Rebuilt units: near-new $/kVA.
  - Substation and power units: median quoted lead time ~130 weeks [calc-price-medians, calc-substation-lt-median].

  Round 1's '<20-week bids' walk-away trigger has not tripped for 3-phase units.
- JST Power Equipment is a subsidiary of China's Hainan Jinpan (P, Jinpan annual report), not a private domestic maker, and it also owns a Lake Mary FL switchgear plant [jst-is-jinpan-subsidiary, jst-lake-mary-plant]. ### Numbers used (computed unless noted)
- **765 kV banks:** 3 sites x 3 banks + 9 sites x 2 banks = 27; x3 single-phase units = 81, plus ~12 spares. Hillje's rating x27 = ~65 GVA.
- **GSUs:** 220 GW / 0.457-0.205 GW per turbine = 481-1,073 turbines; x1.0-1.5 GSUs each = 481-1,610 worldwide. At a ~50% US share, ~240-800 US units over ~5-6 years.
- **Data centers:** 1 GW at 0.9 power factor = 1,111 MVA.
  - MV/LV units: 463-889 per GW (N+1 to 2N redundancy).
  - HV/MV units: 9-22 per GW.
  - ERCOT's 9.456 GW approved: 4,378-8,406 MV/LV units over ~4 years.
  - PJM's +65.7 GW at a 50% data-center share over 10 years: 1,521-2,920 MV/LV units and 30-72 HV/MV units a year.
- **NREL:** stock of 60-80M units / 30-40 year life = 1.5-2.7M units a year.
- **Labor:** 4,388 announced jobs at seven plants vs 3,600 coil winders in NAICS 3353 = 1.22x.

- **Toward buying or converting:** incentives and federal money are too small to change the economics of a greenfield plant.

**Read [margin-direction]:**
1. **The profit pool is large.** Scaled makers earn 16-25% EBITDA, so a newcomer can price under them and still make money.
2. **Gains come from operating leverage and, in power units, from price and mix.** Distribution gross margins are flat to down. Round 1's illustrative squeeze (materials +10-14 points against prices +8%) shows up only as Forgent's ~1.8-point gross-margin slip, so far.
3. **Watch for a turn.** Hammond's falling backlog and Canadian price competition, plus Forgent's capacity headroom, are the first signs that distribution and dry-type could soften.

### What to underwrite
- **Base case:** mid-single-digit nominal price growth plus pass-through of copper, GOES and oil, in steps.
- **Downside:** the 2023-mid-2025 plateau of ~2-3% a year.
- **Entrant margins (judgment, not evidence):** gross ~25-30% and EBITDA ~12-18% after ramp, below the incumbents' 31-36% gross and 16-25% EBITDA.

## Trade, tariffs and the EO

### What the unit math says by segment
- **EHV / 765 kV:** under ~150 units over 2029-34 across the three big plans. That fits inside Hyosung's 130 -> 250+ units/yr and HD Hyundai's +50% EHV, plus imports. The market is lumpy, needs qualification, and belongs to incumbents.
- **GSU:** ~50-160 US units a year from gas, plus hydro replacements and BESS. Large-frame GSUs are LPT-class. Industrial and aeroderivative turbines (Siemens booked 48 industrial units in one quarter) imply a stream of ~30-100 MVA GSUs that a medium-power line could later serve.
- **Data-center MV/LV (2.5-3 MVA):** ERCOT's approved load spread over ~4 years (~1,100-2,100 a year) plus PJM at a 50% data-center share (~1,500-2,900 a year) gives **~2,600-5,000 units a year** in the two biggest markets (computed, W).
  - Stated new supply alone is JST +850 and Giga 1,000-3,000 (~1,850-3,850 a year).
  - Eaton, ERMCO, PTT's pad-mount plant, Eaglerise and Forgent's headroom come on top, unstated.
  - Supply additions are keeping pace with this demand.
- **Data-center HV/MV:** ~50-125 units a year in ERCOT plus PJM (computed). Many campus units are >100 MVA (LPT class), so the 10-60 MVA band leans on utility distribution substations, industrial loads and solar/BESS collector substations. None of those has a unit count yet.
- **Distribution:** replacement alone implies 1.5-2.7M units a year. NREL expects average unit size to rise 120-150%, so capacity grows much faster than unit count. That favors larger 3-phase units over single-phase pole-mounts.

### Demand caveats
- **Queues:** ERCOT's 474 GW queue is more than 5x its record peak, and Texas paused and audited data-center interconnections [ercot-large-load-queue]. PJM trimmed its near-term forecast after vetting large loads.
- **Policy and procurement risk:** ALJs recommended denying some Permian lines [puct-permian-ccn-aug2026], and competitive bidding could re-open ERCOT procurement [mgrid-competitive-bid, W].
- **BESS:** delays under the EO defer GSU and collector-substation orders [bnef-storage-risk].

### Valuation discipline
- **Pay for 2023-25 economics:** underwrite at 2023-25 price growth, ~2-3% a year, not 2026's.
- **Price ceiling:** ≤~8-10x forward EBITDA on that basis. The 12.9-14.1x Prolec multiple reflects scale, ~25% margins and a $5B backlog; a sub-scale plant should clear near the 5.9-8.8x lower-middle-market range plus a scarcity premium.
- **Timing a Central Moloney approach:** Wind Point is in year 3 of a typical 4-6 year hold, so an exit is likely in 2027-29 (inference). It would give a qualified distribution base plus a Florida pole-mount site to add 3-phase and substation lines.
- **Capital-light option:** Giga's deposit-funded growth on ~$30M of equity shows a data-center-anchored entry can run with modest equity, if customers prepay.

## Entry playbook with walk-away criteria

**One-line recommendation:**
- **Build:** a 69-138 kV, 10-60 MVA substation-transformer line (the value driver), on top of a 3-phase pad-mount base (the volume and cash driver).
- **How:** acquire a qualified plant or a distribution maker, or convert an existing building. No greenfield.
- **When:** commit after DOE's EO rules (~Dec 24-27, 2026) and the Q3 margin prints. Ship pad-mounts in H1 2028 and qualified substation units in 2028-29.
- **Avoid:** LPT/EHV, large GSUs and single-phase.

### 1. Products

**Phase 1: 3-phase pad-mounts and unit substations, 1.5-5 MVA, 15-35 kV class.**
- Customers: data centers, utilities and solar/BESS sites.
- Pricing today: ~$28-32/kVA at 1.5-2.5 MVA [calc-price-medians].
- Shelter: 25% tariff against imports. No EO premium.
- The aim is cash flow and a trained workforce, not the long-run margin.

**Phase 2: 10-60 MVA substation units, 69-138 kV primary, optional load tap changer.**
- Uses: utility distribution substations, data-center main substations and solar/BESS collector substations.
- EO 14421 covers this class with no MVA floor [bps-def-69kv].
- Median quoted lead time is ~130 weeks [calc-substation-lt-median, W].
- Korean units below 60 MVA pay no antidumping duty, only the 15% Annex III rate through 2027.

**Later option:** GSUs up to ~100 MVA for industrial and aeroderivative turbines, hydro and BESS. This needs a proven test bay first.

**Avoid:**
- LPT/EHV: under ~150 765 kV units over 2029-34, all going to incumbents.
- Large GSUs.
- Single-phase: loosening, with 6-13 week quotes.

### 2. Route, in order of preference
1. **Acquire a qualified medium-power maker** if one is available at ≤~10x forward EBITDA on 2023-25 price growth. Few are: check Meta Power, VTC's price and Canadian or Mexican independents.
2. **Acquire a qualified distribution maker** and add a 69-138 kV line with a test bay. Budget ~$100-140M or ~$0.7-1.4M per unit/yr of capacity [se-howard-ratings, prolec-gso-deadline, ecida-niagara-older]. Central Moloney is the obvious watch item (Wind Point exit 2027-29).
3. **Buy or lease an existing building** for phase 1: ~$161/sq ft for a finished shell (ERMCO), or ~$25M to fit out a leased 140k sq ft building (Central Moloney). License or JV a phase-2 design with a Korean, Indian or European OEM, and check that the partner is not a covered foreign entity.
4. **No greenfield.** A $340-530M plant shipping in 2029+ lands in the wave and takes on labor-market risk.

### 3. Site
- **Labor first.** Avoid commuting range of the 2027-28 hiring ramps:
  - South Boston VA, Muscle Shoals AL, Jonesville SC, Memphis TN, Montgomery AL, Rincon GA, Goldsboro and Raeford NC.
  - Phoenix's West Valley, where ERMCO hires 500+ in 2027.
- **Great Lakes / Ohio Valley:**
  - Chicago had the deepest winder pool found outside the Southeast (570, 2021).
  - Close to Cliffs Butler, Worthington cores and PJM/MISO demand.
  - New York showed the richest non-federal package found (Niagara ~9.4% of capex with NYPA).
- **Texas:** close to ERCOT load, but competes with Giga, VanTran and Eaton Nacogdoches for labor.
- **Incentives are tie-breakers** at 0.8-6.4% of capex. Budget average wages of ~$65-72k and coil-winder pay of ~$22-27/hr.

### 5. Illustrative economics (assumptions, not evidence)
- **Phase 1:**
  - Volume and revenue: 1,000-1,500 units a year at 2.5 MVA and $28-32/kVA is ~$70-120M of revenue.
  - Capex: ~$60-150M, building plus equipment (ERMCO shell; Howard's equipment line of $45M).
  - EBITDA: at a 12-18% entrant margin, ~$8-22M a year.
- **Phase 2:**
  - Volume: 50-100 units a year at $0.5-1.5M each (anchored on municipal datapoints of $150k-$1.42M) is ~$25-150M of revenue.
  - Capex and staffing: ~$70-140M, with ~80-160 staff at Niagara's 0.62 units per employee.
  - Margin: the higher of the two phases, if power-maker margins hold.

### 6. Customers and contracts
- **Data-center developers:** buy direct and will prepay (Giga model).
- **Utilities:** IOU frame agreements; municipal utilities and co-ops through joint-action agencies and distributor line cards (Wesco, Resco, Irby, Gresco, Border States, Anixter, Sourcewell).
- **Solar/BESS EPCs:** collector substations.
- **Federal:** WAPA and USACE reach mostly through installing integrators.
- **Contract terms:**
  - Index to LME copper, steel and fluid wherever possible.
  - Firm-price municipal business needs hedges.
  - Loss-evaluated bids reward efficient design.

### 7. Core and labor plan
- **Cores:** two North American core suppliers, and design for both GOES and amorphous. Do not build a strip-cutting line until the 7225.11 rate and the core annex are confirmed. Ask Cliffs for an allocation.
- **Labor:**
  - Train winders through a rewind or reman shop before the plant opens. Rebuilt units sell at near-new $/kVA, so the shop pays for itself.
  - Use state pre-hire programs where they exist (the readySC model).

## Implications

- **Opportunity: 69-138 kV substation units are where scarcity, EO coverage and pricing power overlap.** Three findings point to the same band:
  - EO 14421 reaches equipment at ≥69 kV with no MVA floor [bps-def-69kv].
  - Municipal quotes for substation units run ~130 weeks [calc-substation-lt-median, W].
  - Power-transformer makers show the clearest price-led margin gains [hdhe-q2, gev-elec-q2].

  A plant that can type-test and qualify 10-60 MVA, 69-138 kV units by 2028-29 sells into the tightest segment an entrant can reach.
- **Risk: Pad-mount is filling faster than any other segment and gets no EO shelter.** New supply is arriving on several fronts:
  - PTT's pad-mount plant (early 2027), JST/Jinpan (1,000/yr), Giga, Eaton, ERMCO and Eaglerise all add capacity.
  - Forgent claims capacity of ~2x its FY27 guide [ptt-raeford-padmount, fps-mix-capacity].

  Stated additions roughly match the computed data-center pull in ERCOT and PJM. EO 14421 excludes local distribution.
- **Tailwind: Incumbent margins show a real profit pool.** EBITDA and operating margins at scale makers run 16-25% and rose through Q2 2026: Forgent 24.4%, HD Hyundai 25.1%, Hyosung 15.7%, GE Vernova Electrification 18.4% and Hammond 16.4% [margin-direction]. An entrant can price under incumbents and still earn a return.
- **Risk: Gross margins are not widening; Q3 is the first full input-cost test.** Forgent's gross margin slipped ~1.8 points, Hammond's was flat and Hubbell Utility's margin was flat as inflation and tariffs offset price [fps-gross-margin-computed, hubb-us-q2]. Copper (+45%) and base oil (+111%) hit fixed-price backlogs in Q3, with results due late October to November.
- **Risk: Chinese-owned and Korean suppliers already compete inside the US.** The competition is already local:
  - Eaglerise underbid US distributors by 18-23% at Lafayette.
  - Kerec, Sungjin and Taihan hold places on Austin Energy's $200M contracts.
  - JST sells ~$360M a year (annualized) through its US entity [bid-lafayette-2026, bid-austin-200m-2026, jst-usa-h1-2026-financials].

  The 25% tariff does not touch their US-built units.
- **Opportunity: Incumbent mid-power schedules are slipping.** Several 10-160 MVA additions are later or smaller than announced, which widens the 2027-29 window for a qualified newcomer:
  - Gallman partly replaces Crystal Springs and arrives in 2029.
  - Rincon slipped to +50% by March 2027.
  - Muscle Shoals shows no site work.
  - Niagara is pre-construction.

  [se-gallman-replaces-crystal-springs, se-vtc-rincon-slip, se-vtc-ms-scope, niagara-ecida]
- **Risk: Howard and Prolec are taking the 10-100 MVA band on schedule.** Howard Ellisville reaches 250 MVA/345 kV by end-2026, and Prolec Goldsboro doubles medium-power output to 420 units/yr by end-2027 [se-howard-ratings, prolec-gso-deadline]. Win frame agreements in 2027-28 while quotes are long.
- **Neutral: EO-driven replacement of installed Chinese units is unquantified upside.** DOE may order monitoring, isolation or removal of equipment installed before Aug 26 [installed-equipment]. If it acts on 2024-26 Chinese substation units, replacement demand would add to 69 kV+ orders. No estimate exists, so keep this out of the base case.
- **Risk: No federal money in the base case.** None of the federal channels is open to a new builder:
  - No DPA Title III solicitation or award six months after PD 2026-10; authority may have lapsed Sep 30, 2026 (W).
  - 48C is exhausted.
  - EDF has not lent to a grid-equipment maker.
  - There is no reserve.

  [fed-dpa-no-solicitation, fed-dpa-machinery-sunset, fed-48c-exhausted, fed-edf-no-grid-maker, fed-reserve-none]
- **Neutral: Incentives are a tie-breaker; labor and buildings decide sites.** State and local awards come to 0.8-6.4% of capex and pay out on performance over up to 12 years [computed-incentive-intensity]. The only double-digit package (Siemens, ~17%) relied on 48C, which is gone.
- **Risk: Labor sets the pace of any ramp.** Seven plants hiring in 2027-28 announce 4,388 jobs, 1.2x the 3,600 coil winders in all of NAICS 3353. Contracted wages run $65-72k, and Hitachi is building worker housing [announced-jobs-7-plants, computed-wage-benchmarks, hitachi-sb-hiring]. Site away from these ramps, for example in the Chicago or Great Lakes pool.
- **Opportunity: Existing buildings cut time and capex.** Recent entrants show the range:
  - ERMCO bought a finished 566k sq ft shell for $91M (~$161/sq ft).
  - Central Moloney produces in a leased 140k sq ft building on ~$25M.
  - VanTran converted a 430k sq ft distribution center for $20-33M.

  Spec space is still marketed in the West Valley [ermco-az-91m, se-cmi-operating, tx-vantran-waco, waddell-spec-shells].
- **Risk: Core costs may be higher than modelled.** One reading of the April annex puts cores and laminations at 50%, not 25% [verify-s232-cores]. At 50%, cores consume ~10 of the 15 points of large-unit tariff shelter (computed). Butler's new GOES does not land until 2028 [verify-cliffs-butler].
- **Opportunity: Aftermarket earns a speed premium and trains labor.** Rebuilt pad-mounts sell at near-new $/kVA with 4-12 week delivery, and emergency units carry a ~33% premium [bid-coldwater-rebuilt-2025, bid-niles-2025, lt-reman-testimony-2026]. A reman shop is a cash-generating on-ramp for coil winders.
- **Neutral: The valuation gap rewards buy-and-build, if targets exist.** Scale cleared at ~12.9-14.1x EBITDA (Prolec), against 5.9-8.8x for lower-middle-market manufacturing [prolec-implied-multiple, lmm-manufacturing-multiples]. But Southwest Electric, Delta Star, PTT and Sunbelt Solomon are taken. Central Moloney (Wind Point, exit window 2027-29) is the most actionable target.
- **Risk: Policy volatility persists.** IEEPA and Section 122 tariffs were overturned. Annex III's 15% tier expires Dec 31, 2027, DPA may have lapsed, and the EO's licensing design is unknown. Any tariff-dependent margin is a policy bet.

## Method and limits

- Research agents could only read pages through web search. This environment's network policy blocks direct access to census.gov, bls.gov, sec.gov, regulations.gov and state permit portals, so long PDFs and data tables were read through search summaries.
- Web search is capped at 200 calls per turn across all agents. The first round ran out early, so a second round re-ran the domains that had no data, with fixed allowances per agent.
- Plant online dates are company guidance unless the plant-status table cites a permit or incentive record.
