# The transformer build-out: what it means for Fulton Asset Management

**FAM should profit from the grid build-out by supplying metals, parts and repair work to the existing transformer fleet, starting with a retired-transformer recovery program at Imperial and a regional repair-and-rebuild platform, not by building transformers.**

The transformer shortage is real, but it now differs by segment. Substation and power units still quote about 130 weeks. New 3-phase pad-mount capacity is filling fast in 2027-28 (PTT, JST, Giga, Eaton, ERMCO, Eaglerise, Forgent), and single-phase supply is loosening. FAM does not need to build transformers to benefit. A qualified 69-138 kV line costs about $100-140M plus a test bay. That is above FAM's observed deal sizes and outside its skills. FAM's edge is metals and operations. Imperial already recovers copper and aluminum from cable. Metaltec, SFE, Panther and AFCO make consumables and parts. Transend runs Mexico freight lanes.

The plan has three layers:
1. Low-cost moves inside the existing companies, starting now. Imperial, Exeon and Midwest Industrial Metals run a program to recover retired transformers and factory scrap, with Transend doing pickup. SFE, Metaltec, AFCO and Panther qualify with named transformer makers and component makers.
2. One platform acquisition. Buy a founder-owned regional shop that repairs and rewinds transformers. Utilities pay near-new prices for rebuilt units delivered in 4-12 weeks. Further shops can be bought at lower-middle-market multiples of about 6-9x EBITDA.
3. Selective add-ons for the still-tight power segment. Radiators and heavy fabrication, and aluminum castings for bushings and tap changers.

Tie growth to the existing fleet of 60-80M transformers, not to the new-plant cycle. That cycle risks too much pad-mount capacity in 2027-28 and looser mid-power supply in 2029-30. All addressable-market figures are estimates, not current sales.

## Why this fits how Fulton invests

This plan suits FAM's family-capital, metals-operator model.

Size. Each play is a lower-middle-market industrial business next to Imperial's metal flows: scrap, castings, fabrication, consumables and freight. None needs a test bay, utility type-testing or a $100M+ plant.

Permanent capital. The two lead plays depend on the existing transformer fleet (60-80M units; about 1.5-2.7M replacements a year, computed from NREL). They do not depend on the new-plant cycle. That makes them steadier, which suits holding with no planned exit. The new-plant cycle points to pad-mount overbuild in 2027-28 and mid-power loosening in 2029-30.

Check sizes (inferred from limited deal data).
• Evidence: FAM SFE Holdings raised $6.0M of equity (Form D, 2024). Mayco was bought for $31.3M (c.2012-13). Panther is about $5.4M of revenue (weak source). AFCO paid $65.5M to only 231 of its 373 ESOP participants, so AFCO was likely around $100M or more of enterprise value (inference).
• Suggested ranges:
• Projects inside existing companies: about $1-10M.
• Add-ons: about $5-30M of enterprise value.
• One platform: about $15-80M of enterprise value, co-sponsored with Ashland and/or Lorentzen and financed with bank debt plus mezzanine, as with Metaltec and AFCO.

Valuation. Pay about 6-9x EBITDA. Walk above about 9-10x on 2023-25 economics. The 12.9-14.1x paid for Prolec reflects scale, and FAM does not plan to sell, so it cannot cash in that gap. A cheap entry price only lowers risk.

Deal type. Target founder-succession and ESOP-liquidity sellers (FAM's pattern at AFCO, SFE and Metaltec), not PE auctions.

Light integration. FAM runs its companies as separate units. The cross-company links (Imperial with Transend, Metaltec, SFE and Panther) need one program coordinator and shared customer lists, not mergers.

## Fulton at a glance

Fulton Asset Management (FAM) is a Chicago family office and investment platform. It buys and builds lower-middle-market industrial businesses with permanent family capital: no fund structure and no planned exit (fultonasset.com). FAM is the investment arm of the family behind The Imperial Group, a vertically integrated Chicago metals recycling, alloying and manufacturing group founded in 1962. Imperial is headquartered at 1700 W Fulton St, which likely explains the 'Fulton' name (inference). The Aug 2025 AFCO release says FAM owns and operates 'over 20 operations', including Imperial Group, Mayco Industries, Stainless Foundry & Engineering, Metaltec Steel Abrasives, Setna iO, Panther Metalflow, Transend Logistics and AFCO Industries. FAM rarely invests alone. Its recurring co-sponsors are Ashland Capital Partners (Chicago; owns nine lower-middle-market industrial businesses) and Lorentzen Investments, with Atlas Capital Ventures listed on Mayco. The identified portfolio clusters in non-ferrous and ferrous scrap recycling (copper and aluminum wire and cable), zinc, aluminum and lead alloys and mill products, castings, steel abrasives, CNC machining and fabrication, aviation aftermarket, and 3PL trucking. None of the identified holdings makes electrical equipment or transformers.

Family-office 'buy and build' with permanent capital and an operator background. (1) It is led by the operating family of a metals recycler and alloy producer, so the companies it buys tend to sit next to Imperial's metal flows: scrap steel feeds Metaltec's shot and grit; zinc die casting at Panther Metalflow sits beside Imperial Zinc; lead rolling at Mayco; copper and aluminum wire recovery at Exeon and Midwest Industrial Metals. (2) It co-sponsors with Chicago family offices (Ashland, Lorentzen) and finances with senior bank debt plus mezzanine (CIBC, Fifth Third, Graycliff). (3) It buys succession and liquidity situations: AFCO's ESOP repurchase strain, SFE's long-time owner exiting, and Metaltec's PE fund forced to divest. (4) Incumbent management usually stays. (5) Add-ons and international expansion happen inside holdings, as with Setna iO buying a majority of J&C Aero in 2026. (6) Smaller stakes, such as ZYCI, add capital for equipment, people and capacity. (7) It holds indefinitely with no exit horizon. Integration looks light: companies are run as separate units with no evidence of merging them into one operation.

## Plays across the value chain, ranked

### 1. Recover metal from retired transformers and from transformer-factory scrap

*S3, scrap and recycling. Feeds S2 (copper rod mills, scrap-fed steel mills) and draws scrap from C1 utilities and B-layer builders. · conviction High*

- **Why Fulton:** This is FAM's home business.
• Existing capability: Imperial, Exeon and Midwest Industrial Metals already granulate cable and recover copper and aluminum at three sites in the Chicago area and Indiana.
• Steady feedstock: the installed base is 60-80M units, with about 1.5-2.7M replaced a year (computed). Volumes do not depend on the new-plant cycle.
• Copper economics: copper at about $14,474/t (+45%) and refined-copper duties of 15% (2027) and 30% (2028) raise the value of domestic scrap.
• Possible upside: DOE may order installed Chinese bulk-power units removed (not quantified).
• Addressable market (est., not sales, overlapping): $30-120M/yr for retired-unit buyback (f2) and $20-80M/yr for closed-loop copper chops (f1).
- **How:** Start inside the existing companies, then add one acquisition.
• Production scrap: buyback agreements with transformer and magnet-wire plants within trucking range (Prolec GE and Eaton in Waukesha WI, WEG Washington MO, Howard MS, ERMCO).
• Retired units: a buyback and teardown pilot for Midwest utilities, co-ops and municipal utilities.
• Logistics: Transend handles pickup.
• Add-on: buy a regional dismantler that already holds oil-processing permits.
- **Check size:** $2-8M for the pilot: teardown line, oil handling, permits and working capital (estimate). Add-on at $10-40M of enterprise value, about 5-7x EBITDA (inferred).
- **Targets:** A-Line and TCI of Ohio appear in the research as transformer recyclers; their ownership and whether they would sell were not checked. Archetype: a regional transformer dismantler with oil-processing and PCB permits. Sunbelt Solomon recycles about 1,000 units a day and is pending sale to TJC. It is a competitor, but could also be a scrap partner.
- **Timing:** Start now. Pilot running in Q1-Q2 2027. Add-on decision by mid-2027.
- **Risks:** • A copper price reversal.
• Competition from Sunbelt Solomon and established recyclers.
• Many retired units go to rebuilders, not recyclers.
• Environmental liability for oil and PCBs (from general knowledge).
• Southwire's use of scrap feed is unconfirmed.
• The f2 market overlaps the existing scrap pool, so it is not all new volume.

### 2. Regional transformer repair, rewind and rebuild platform

*B layer, aftermarket and remanufacturing. Regional shops est. $350-850M/yr; Sunbelt Solomon est. $150-350M. · conviction Medium*

- **Why Fulton:** Utilities pay for speed.
• Price and delivery: rebuilt pad-mounts sell at near-new prices ($18-34/kVA) and ship in 4-12 weeks, against 40-50 weeks for new 3-phase pad-mounts and about 130 weeks for substation units. Emergency units carry about a 33% premium.
• Seller fit: founder-owned shops are the succession sales FAM already buys (AFCO's ESOP, SFE's retiring owner).
• Labor: Chicago has the deepest coil-winder pool found outside the Southeast (570 winders in 2021).
• Cross-company links: scrap goes to Imperial, freight to Transend, and parts can come from SFE and Panther.
• Durable demand: work follows the installed base, not new-plant capacity.
- **How:** Buy one founder-owned regional shop as a platform (Midwest or Plains), then add 2-4 shops. Concentrate on 3-phase pad-mounts and small substation units, roughly up to 20-30 MVA (judgment), where new-unit lead times stay long. Avoid single-phase rebuilds, where supply is loosening. Co-sponsor with Ashland or Lorentzen.
- **Check size:** Platform: $15-60M of enterprise value, about 6-8x EBITDA of an estimated $2-8M. Add-ons: $5-20M each. All figures inferred.
- **Targets:** Midwest Electric Transformers (Hesston KS; appears to be the same firm as Midwest Electric Transformer Service), T&R Electric (supplied Niles MI) and K P Electric (supplied Independence MO). The research names them only as suppliers; ownership and sale interest were not checked. Already taken or rival bidders: Sunbelt Solomon (pending to TJC), IPS/Belyea (Oct 7, 2026) and Voltaris (Mill Point).
- **Timing:** Target list in Q4 2026. LOI in H1 2027. Close by end-2027, while substation lead times are long (tight through 2028).
- **Risks:** • PE roll-ups (TJC, IPS, Mill Point) bid up targets.
• No margin data was found for this segment.
• The speed premium fades as pad-mount supply grows (2027-28) and mid-power loosens (2029-30).
• New plants compete for winders (4,388 announced jobs against 3,600 winders nationwide).
• Oil and PCB liability.
• Dependence on the founder.

### 3. Castings and turned parts for bushings, tap changers, valves and monitoring devices

*S1. Foundries and machine shops (est. $140-450M/yr) supplying the tap-changer and bushing makers (Reinhausen, Trench, Hitachi components, Qualitrol). · conviction Medium*

- **Why Fulton:** Bushings and tap changers are bottleneck components, and their makers are localizing.
• Reinhausen: Humboldt TN +45%; global capacity to double by 2030; many castings still come from Germany.
• Trench: new Charlotte NC bushing plant opened Jul 2026, 3,500 units a year expandable to 10,000.
• Hubbell PCORE: another US bushing maker.
• FAM fit: SFE already serves power-generation OEMs with 6-week lead times. Panther turns parts and casts zinc around inserts using Imperial Zinc metal. A foundry add-on could take Imperial Aluminum alloy (f3 est. $10-45M addressable).
- **How:** First, qualify SFE and Panther with these customers. Then buy an aluminum or bronze sand or permanent-mold foundry that makes bushing flanges, terminal pads and tank fittings, supplied with Imperial Aluminum alloy.
- **Check size:** Under $2M to qualify existing companies (samples, approvals, tooling; estimate). Foundry add-on: $5-30M of enterprise value (inferred).
- **Targets:** Customers: Reinhausen (Humboldt TN), Trench (Charlotte NC), Hubbell PCORE (LeRoy NY), Hitachi Energy, Qualitrol, Eaton. Acquisition archetype: a Midwest aluminum foundry, family-owned with a succession need, that already has electrical-hardware customers. No named targets were found.
- **Timing:** RFQs in Q4 2026-Q1 2027. First-article orders in 2027. Foundry add-on in 2027-28.
- **Risks:** • The direct pools are small: f6 $1-5M, f7 $2-10M, f10 $1-6M (estimates).
• Qualification takes 12-24 months (judgment).
• European makers may keep sourcing from home.
• Hitachi's components are captive.
• Foundry labor and environmental compliance.
• Customers may integrate the work themselves.

### 4. Radiators, coolers and heavy fabricated parts for substation and power units

*S1, tanks, radiators and fabrication. Bought-out radiators and coolers est. $150-400M/yr. · conviction Medium*

- **Why Fulton:** This serves the segment that stays tight: substation and power units at about 130 weeks.
• Bought out: radiators are 3-5% of a power unit's price, and 60-85% are bought from outside suppliers.
• Supply: Trantech is the leading North American maker; the rest come from Menk and Mexican, Korean and Indian makers. Imported transformer parts pay 15% under Section 232 (how radiators are classified was not confirmed).
• Metals fit: radiators are formed, welded, blasted and galvanized or painted steel. That draws on fabrication skills, Imperial Zinc and Metaltec.
• Example demand: radiator purchases by VTC alone are est. $20-60M/yr.
- **How:** Buy an existing radiator or cooler maker, or a heavy-fabrication shop that already makes conservators and radiator headers. A licensed radiator line at AFCO is a fallback, only with a signed builder offtake.
- **Check size:** $20-80M of enterprise value as a co-sponsored platform (estimate). A line at AFCO would cost about $5-15M (judgment) and only proceeds with signed offtake.
- **Targets:** Trantech Radiator Products is named in the research as the leading North American panel-radiator maker; ownership and availability are unknown. Archetypes: small US or Mexican panel-radiator and cooler makers. Buyers to interview: VTC, WEG, Delta Star/MacLean, SGB-SMIT/Southwest Electric, Howard (medium power), PTT/Niagara.
- **Timing:** Market check in Q4 2026-Q1 2027. Act in 2027 to sell into 2027-29 frame agreements. Do not add new capacity that arrives in 2029-30.
- **Risks:** • Mid-power supply loosens in 2029-30 (Gallman, Muscle Shoals).
• Builders bring radiators in-house.
• The target pool is thin.
• Customer qualification takes time.
• Tariff changes: the Annex III tier ends Dec 2027, and part rates could change.
• Labor competition in the Southeast.

### 5. Enclosures and sub-assemblies that do not hold pressure, made at AFCO for Southeast builders

*S1, independent tank and enclosure fabricators. Outsourced work est. $170-800M of a $1.4-3.0B tank pool. · conviction Medium*

- **Why Fulton:** AFCO's plants in LA, MS, AL and IL are in the Southeast transformer cluster, and it already does CNC fabrication and contract manufacturing. Addressable est. $3-20M at Howard (f8) and $3-25M at Eaton (f9). Work can start without new capex.
- **How:** Win contract work inside AFCO first: cabinet doors, sills, platforms, guards, brackets, and aluminum or stainless enclosures. Buy a fabricator that can make sealed tanks only if a builder commits multi-year volume.
- **Check size:** $1-5M of tooling and fixtures inside AFCO, only against purchase orders. Tank-fabricator add-on: $10-40M of enterprise value (inferred).
- **Targets:** Customers: Howard (MS), Prolec Shreveport (LA), HD Hyundai Montgomery (AL), Hitachi Crystal Springs (MS), Eaton (Nacogdoches TX; Jonesville SC from 2027). Washington Metal Fabricators and Acero ATD are named in the research as contract tank fabricators; their ownership was not checked. Archetype: a Gulf South welding shop with pressure-vessel experience.
- **Timing:** Quotes now through Q1 2027. Keep it to contract work only, because 2027 is crowded for pad-mounts.
- **Risks:** • Pad-mount overbuild in 2027-28 (PTT, JST, Giga, Eaton, ERMCO, Eaglerise, Forgent).
• Most builders make tanks in-house.
• Chinese-owned and Korean price pressure (18-23% underbids) flows down to suppliers.
• New plants compete for welders.
• AFCO is still recent (Jul 2025 close).

### 6. Cross-border and reverse logistics for distribution-size units and components (Transend)

*S1, logistics, flatbed and cross-border only (not heavy haul). Addressable est. $10-40M (f11) and $12-45M (f12). · conviction Medium*

- **Why Fulton:** Transend already runs flatbed and Mexico-US-Canada lanes.
• Mexico flows: Mexico supplied 45% of 2019 US core imports and hosts Prolec Monterrey, Magnekon, Corefficient and Chinese-owned plants (Eaglerise and Jinpan Mexico).
• Reverse logistics: Transend can collect retired units for Play 1 and haul scrap to Imperial.
• Cost: almost no capital needed.
- **How:** Organic sales and carrier development. Optionally, add a step-deck or RGN flatbed carrier that holds oversize permits.
- **Check size:** Under $2M inside Transend (estimate). Optional carrier add-on: $5-15M of enterprise value (inferred).
- **Targets:** Shippers: Prolec GE (Monterrey to US), Worthington's Corefficient (Monterrey cores), Magnekon (Monterrey magnet wire), importers of distribution units. Archetype add-on: a Midwest or Texas step-deck carrier.
- **Timing:** Now. Recheck after the USMCA review in Jul 2027.
- **Risks:** • Brokerage margins are thin.
• Importers often ship delivered-duty-paid with their own carriers.
• USMCA and tariff changes.
• Freight cycles.
• Possible reputational or EO exposure when hauling for Chinese-owned shippers.

### 7. Hot-dip galvanizing for transmission structures and substation steel (zinc vertical)

*Grid build-out outside the transformer value chain, downstream of Imperial Zinc. Not covered in the value-chain research. · conviction Low*

- **Why Fulton:** Galvanizing is a metals business that suits permanent capital.
• Zinc link: Imperial Zinc already makes galvanizing alloys and anodes.
• Long-lived assets: galvanizing kettles last a long time, which suits a no-exit holder.
• Demand: large transmission programs need galvanized steel: ERCOT 765 kV plan (about 2,468 mi, $32.99B), MISO Tranche 2.1 (3,631 mi, $21.8B) and PJM 765 kV (about 770 mi across two windows).
- **How:** Screen the market first, then buy one regional galvanizer as a platform or as an add-on to Imperial Zinc.
- **Check size:** $15-60M of enterprise value (estimate; no market data in the research).
- **Targets:** Archetype: an independent Midwest or Southern galvanizer that serves tower, pole and substation-steel fabricators. Start with Imperial Zinc's existing customers.
- **Timing:** Screen in 2027. Act in 2027-28, ahead of transmission construction (ERCOT Eastern 2030-32; MISO 2032-34).
- **Risks:** • No research covers galvanizing margins, competitors or targets.
• Transmission projects slip, as when judges recommended denying some Permian lines.
• Environmental exposure from acid and zinc ash.
• Energy costs.
• Large incumbents have scale.

### 8. Independent supplier of insulation kits and pressboard components

*S1, insulation. Converters, kits and imports outside Weidmann est. $250-640M/yr. · conviction Low*

- **Why Fulton:** Insulation kits are a named bottleneck. Hitachi bought Canduct (London ON, Boardman OH) on Jul 2, 2026, so rival builders (VTC, Howard, WEG, Prolec, the Korean-owned US plants) may want a supplier that a competitor does not own. Machining pressboard is CNC work that FAM's shops understand. The fit with metals is weak, though.
- **How:** Buy a small independent kit converter, or partner with a pressboard mill on a regional kit shop.
- **Check size:** $10-40M of enterprise value (estimate).
- **Targets:** Archetype: an independent North American insulation-kit converter. Weidmann (St. Johnsbury VT) is the main board supplier and a possible partner, not a target. Krempel is named in the research as a converter but is not a likely target.
- **Timing:** Watch only. Revisit in 2027 once there is evidence of rival builders moving away from Canduct.
- **Risks:** • Not a metals business, so FAM has little operating edge.
• Dependence on Weidmann for board.
• Long qualification.
• Thin target pool.
• Hitachi could undercut on price.
• Builders keep integrating components.

## Signals that would change the plan

- **DOE's EO 14421 rules (about Dec 24-27, 2026) order monitoring, isolation or removal of installed Chinese bulk-power units.:** Replacement and teardown demand rises. Speed up Plays 1-2. If instead the rules set a high MVA floor or grandfather existing suppliers broadly, the aftermarket plays are unchanged and the urgency of Plays 3-4 drops.
- **Forgent's gross margin falls below 33%, or Hammond's below 29.5%, for two quarters; or Hammond's backlog falls a second quarter in a row (Q3 prints in Nov 2026).:** Distribution and dry-type are softening. Keep AFCO enclosure work to contract-only, and do not approve pad-mount-related capex.
- **At least 10 bid tabs show a median 3-phase pad-mount delivery under 20 weeks.:** The speed premium on rebuilt pad-mounts is gone. Steer the repair platform toward substation repair and rentals, and lower the price you will pay for targets.
- **Median substation quotes fall below 52 weeks across 10 or more 2027 awards.:** Power-unit scarcity is ending. Slow down the radiator and component acquisitions, and pay only for 2023-25 economics.
- **Copper drops well below about $14,500/t, or the Section 232 refined-copper schedule (15% in 2027, 30% in 2028) is changed.:** Recalculate the teardown and scrap economics. Higher duties favor domestic secondary copper; a price collapse shrinks recycling spreads.
- **The TJC-Sunbelt Solomon close, IPS/Belyea, Voltaris deals, or a 2027-29 sale of Central Moloney show small repair shops trading above about 9x EBITDA.:** Switch from buying to building: open a repair and rewind shop in the Chicago area using the local winder pool, fed by Play 1 scrap.
- **Builders announce in-house tank, radiator or enclosure lines, or buy their suppliers (as Hitachi did with Canduct).:** Downgrade Plays 4-5 and Play 8. Focus on builders that are not integrating, such as VTC, WEG, Delta Star/MacLean and the Korean-owned US plants.
- **The Annex III 15% tier expires on Dec 31, 2027, Section 232 part rates change, or the Jul 2027 USMCA review changes Mexican flows.:** Import competition for radiators and castings shifts, and so do Transend's cross-border volumes. Re-underwrite Plays 3, 4 and 6.
- **Reinhausen (Humboldt TN), Trench (Charlotte NC) or Hubbell PCORE launch local-sourcing programs or announce further expansions.:** Speed up SFE qualification and the foundry add-on in Play 3.
- **Welders or winders leave AFCO or acquired repair shops for new transformer plants (Howard, HD Hyundai, Hitachi Gallman; announced wages of $65-72k).:** Budget for higher wages, or move work to Illinois and the Chicago labor pool. Slow any Southeast expansion.
- **Construction contracts are awarded on MISO Tranche 2.1, ERCOT 765 kV or PJM 765 kV lines.:** Start the galvanizing screen (Play 7) and Imperial Zinc's outreach to galvanizers.
- **Cliffs offers merchant electrical-steel (GOES) allocations after 2028, and imported cores are confirmed at 50% duty.:** Revisit a core-cutting or lamination business, which is currently on the avoid list.

## What to avoid

- Building or buying a transformer plant, including a 69-138 kV line. It needs about $100-140M plus a $10-20M test bay and about 2 years to win utility approval. That is above FAM's observed deal sizes and outside its skills.
- Capex sized to pad-mount or single-phase volumes (cabinet lines, enclosure capacity) without signed orders. Pad-mount capacity is filling in 2027-28, the EO gives distribution no protection, and Chinese-owned and Korean suppliers are underbidding by 18-23%.
- Paying scale multiples for a 'grid' label. Prolec cleared at 12.9-14.1x. Stay near 6-9x and walk away above about 9-10x on 2023-25 economics.
- A core-cutting or lamination service center for now. Cliffs' electrical steel is not available to newcomers, imported strip reportedly pays 50% duty, and the duty on cores is unresolved.
- Heavy haul and Schnabel rail. Both are capital-intensive and held by scale players (Barnhart, Mammoet, Bigge, Emmert, Sarens), and Transend has no such capability.
- Test labs and field installation crews. These are service businesses outside FAM's metals-operator model.
- Businesses that depend on dry-type transformers, which are softening (Hammond's backlog fell 6.9% QoQ), and bets on solid-state transformer startups.
- Business cases that need tariffs or federal money. IEEPA tariffs were struck down, Section 122 was ruled invalid, the Annex III tier ends in Dec 2027, and the base case has no DPA, 48C or EDF money.
- Auctions against PE roll-ups (TJC, IPS, Mill Point) for repair shops. Stick to proprietary succession and ESOP deals.
- Taking retired units or used oil without PCB screening, permits and indemnities. This is a general-knowledge caution, not from the research.
- Relying on a single vertically integrating builder, such as Hitachi, as the only customer of a component line.
- Forcing a grid story onto Mayco, Setna iO or ZYCI.

## Caveats

• FAM's criteria are inferred. Its size criteria, deal sizes and suggested check sizes come from thin evidence: the SFE Form D ($6.0M of equity), the Mayco price ($31.3M, c.2012-13), AFCO's ESOP payout to a subset of participants, and aggregator revenues. FAM's own website and Axial criteria could not be read.
• Portfolio coverage is incomplete. Only about 13 of FAM's 20+ operations were identified, so relevant units may be missing.
• Market sizes are estimates. All Fulton addressable-market figures (about $93-401M a year in total) estimate reachable markets, not current sales, and they overlap with each other.
• Buyer data is weak. Buyer prices and lead times are mostly weak-grade summaries of municipal minutes. No margin data was found for repair and rebuild shops.
• Target names are unverified. Named targets appear in the research as market participants; their ownership and willingness to sell were not checked.
• Some plays sit outside the research. Galvanizing and the Ashland precast idea fall outside the researched value chain. PCB and oil-handling points come from general knowledge. Ashland's holdings are not confirmed as FAM-owned.
• The picture will change soon. Everything reflects the Oct 12, 2026 deep dive, with no new web search. Q3 results, the DOE rules under EO 14421 and the question of whether cores pay 25% or 50% are all pending and could change the ranking.
